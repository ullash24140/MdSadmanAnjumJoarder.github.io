01 / about

From flue gas to fuel

I am a Mechanical Engineer with a B.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from Rajshahi University of Engineering & Technology (RUET), Bangladesh. My research focuses on carbon capture, clean energy technologies, and renewable energy systems.

My undergraduate thesis explored the hydrothermal carbonization of water hyacinth, green algae, and waste tea leaves, converting these abundant waste materials into solid biofuel. That project established the foundation of my research philosophy: transforming undervalued waste streams into sustainable resources through innovative engineering solutions.

Since then, my research has expanded to encompass carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS); solar photovoltaic (PV) techno-economic analysis; renewable energy policy for Bangladesh and Nepal; and biomass and microalgae conversion technologies. My work has been published in reputable international journals, including Environmental Science and Pollution Research, Energy Reviews, IEEE Access, Heliyon, Separation Science and Technology and others.

Alongside my research, I served as an Instructor under the Directorate of Technical Education, where I taught and mentored the next generation of Bangladeshi technicians. I currently work as an Assistant Engineer (Mechanical) in the Roads and Highways Department of Bangladesh. To me, research, teaching, and public infrastructure represent three complementary dimensions of the same mission: applying engineering knowledge to develop practical, sustainable solutions that improve people's lives.

Selected projects

Thesis, industrial placement and the engineering concepts behind the publications.

Undergraduate Thesis · RUET

Hydrothermal carbonization of water hyacinth, green algae & waste tea leaves

Final-year thesis under Prof. Dr. Mohammad Shahed Hasan Khan Tushar. Water hyacinth — an invasive weed choking Bangladesh's waterways — was hydrothermally treated at 260, 280 and 300 °C alongside green algae and waste tea leaves, then characterised by ultimate and proximate analysis, SEM, XRD, TGA/DTG and higher heating value.

Hydrochar and biocrude produced at 300 °C reached higher heating values of 27.3 MJ/kg and 22.98 MJ/kg respectively, with carbon content well above the raw feedstock. Published at ICMERE 2019 and extended into Waste and Biomass Valorization (2025).

Research Concept · 2019 – 2023

Compressor-driven CO₂ and SO₂ capture for industrial workplaces

A compressor-driven system that captures CO₂ and SO₂ from the polluted air of industrial plants and households, then repurposes the separated CO₂ as a fire-extinguishing agent — turning a waste stream into a safety asset while keeping workplace gas concentrations within safe limits.

Developed across three ICMERE/ICMIME conference papers and matured into a peer-reviewed article in Separation Science and Technology (Taylor & Francis, 2023).

Research Concept · 2021

Electricity harvesting from train wheel rotation

A proposed system generating electricity from the rotational speed of train wheels to power fans, lights and internal electrical devices on board — eliminating the need for separate generator compartments and freeing space for passengers.

Full costing of fabricated wheels, motor, battery, copper wire and rotor put annual fuel savings at roughly $35,040, with the initial investment recovered in about 750 days. Presented at ICMERE 2021.

energy-harvestingrailtechno-economics
Simulation Study · 2021 – 2024

Generation III+ reactor transient analysis in PCTRAN

A sustained simulation programme comparing VVER-1200 and AP-1000 reactors under severe transients: loss-of-coolant accidents in the hot leg, turbine trip concurrent with anticipated transient without SCRAM, steam generator tube rupture, and loss of AC power.

Tracked RCS pressure and temperature, void fraction, containment pressure, pressurizer level and DNBR against established benchmarks to identify vulnerabilities and refine emergency response protocols. Four papers across IEEE ACMI and ICMERE.

Simulation Study · 2024 – 2025

Aspen Plus modelling of microalgal biocrude

Hydrothermal liquefaction of Phaeodactylum tricornutum, Spirulina platensis and Chlorella vulgaris simulated in Aspen Plus® at elevated temperature and pressure, avoiding the drying step that makes wet biomass uneconomic.

C. vulgaris gave the highest calorific value at 37.27 MJ/kg (621 K); P. tricornutum gave the best energy recovery at 88.78%. Published in Heliyon (Elsevier, 2024).
